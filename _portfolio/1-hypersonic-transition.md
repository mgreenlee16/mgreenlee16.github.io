## The problem

Predicting where a hypersonic boundary layer transitions from laminar to turbulent is critical to vehicle design. At the same location on the vehicle, heat transfer to the skin is roughly **three to five times higher** when the flow is turbulent than when it is laminar. Transition location therefore drives the design of the thermal protection system.

Real vehicle surfaces are not held at a single uniform temperature. My research asks how **varying wall temperature profiles** change the boundary layer and where it transitions.

## Current work: flared cone

I am now investigating how varying wall temperature affects **the boundary layer as a whole**, not only the transition location, using a **flared cone** geometry.

- Running computational analyses of the flared cone in **STABL**.
- Connecting the computations to **physical experiments in the BAM6QT**, so the predictions can be compared with wind tunnel measurements.

## First study: sharp cone

My first project looked at how varying wall temperature affects boundary-layer transition on a **sharp cone**.

- Ran mean-flow simulations of a sharp cone in hypersonic flight with **DPLR2D**, part of the Stability and Transition Analysis for Hypersonic Boundary Layers (**STABL**) suite.
- Ran linear stability analysis with **PSE-Chem** to track the growth of boundary-layer instabilities under different wall temperature distributions.
- Post-processed and compared results with **MATLAB** scripts and **Tecplot** visualizations.
- Presented the results in my first research talk, *[Impact of Varying Wall Temperature on Hypersonic Boundary-Layer Transition](/talks/2026-07-30-purdue-surf)*, at the 2026 Purdue Summer Undergraduate Research Symposium.
